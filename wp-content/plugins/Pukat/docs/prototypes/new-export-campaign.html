Executive Summary

The phishing simulation campaign was conducted across 200 users spanning 10 departments to evaluate how employees respond to email-based social engineering designed to emulate a malware-delivery scenario.

Of the total population, 37 users (18.5%) performed a click or other interaction classified as risk-bearing. By contrast, 61 users (30.5%) actively reported the simulated email to the security function.

The results indicate that a baseline level of security awareness is evident across part of the population; however, reporting discipline and resistance to phishing remain inconsistent across several business units and warrant targeted reinforcement.

Overall Assessment
Medium
Meaningful user susceptibility remains evident, while active reporting behaviour is not yet consistently embedded across the population.

Summary

Across the 200-user population, 102 users (51.0%) neither clicked nor reported the simulated email. A further 37 users (18.5%) performed an interaction classified as risk-bearing, while 61 users (30.5%) reported the message to the security function. The distribution suggests that active reporting behaviour has begun to take hold, yet it remains insufficiently pervasive; at the same time, the proportion of risk-bearing interactions remains material enough to justify continued, targeted awareness measures.

Recommendations & Follow-up Actions

01
Targeted Awareness for Higher-Susceptibility Business Units
Deliver targeted awareness interventions to business units with the highest rate of risk-bearing interaction, using scenarios that closely reflect their day-to-day operational context and likely threat exposure.
Priority 1
02
Embed Phishing Reporting as a Routine Security Behaviour
Reinforce the designated phishing-reporting channel and measure both the speed and consistency of user reporting in subsequent campaigns.
Priority 1
03
Establish Recurring Simulation and Trend Monitoring
Track click rates, reporting rates, and departmental response patterns across successive campaign cycles to identify sustained improvement, regression, or emerging areas of concern.
